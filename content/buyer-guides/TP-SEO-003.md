# How to Compare Perfume Samples Before a Wholesale Order

A perfume sample can help you decide what belongs in your shop, but a good first impression is only one part of the buying decision. You also need to know which product you received, how you compared it and which details still need a supplier's answer.

Use this guide to turn a shortlist into a recorded comparison. It is a retail buying worksheet, not a laboratory test or a promise about fragrance performance. Start with the products in your [wholesale perfume shortlist](https://topperfume.cn/collections/fragrance), then keep observations separate from the facts supplied with each sample.

## Identify every sample before comparing it

Give each sample a reference that you can use in notes, photographs and follow-up messages. Record the product name, supplier reference, date received and the size stated on the supplied label. If a batch or lot reference is present, copy it exactly; leave missing details unresolved.

Ask what the sample represents. A small fragrance vial may let you explore a scent, while a finished retail unit also lets you inspect its bottle, sprayer and carton. Neither should automatically be treated as approval of every component in a future order.

For example, the [Lattafa Khamrah product page](https://topperfume.cn/products/lattafa-khamrah) identifies a particular branded wholesale product. Ask about a sample of that exact reference and its current availability. It is not a proposal to put your logo on the branded product.

Before paying, confirm the sample reference, quantity, sample charge, shipping charge and destination. Sample availability can be confirmed for the selected product or project. Samples are charged, and shipping is paid by the buyer.

## Separate scent preference from other buying criteria

Avoid reducing the entire decision to one score. A sample may match your shop's scent direction but have unresolved packaging details. Another may be well presented yet overlap too closely with a product you already sell.

| Comparison area | What to record | What the note does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scent direction | Your own description and the customer preference it might suit | A verified ingredient list or an official scent-note specification |
| Perceived intensity | How noticeable it seems under the conditions you record | A universal projection or strength rating |
| Change over time | Your observations at named times during the comparison | A guaranteed wear duration for every customer |
| Spray and handling | What you observe from the supplied sprayer, cap and bottle | The condition of an entire future shipment |
| Packaging | Visible condition, supplied components and label references | Confirmation of uninspected packaging or a custom version |
| Commercial fit | The questions about quantity, cost and replenishment that remain | Current stock, an agreed bulk quote or a delivery commitment |

## Keep comparison conditions as consistent as practical

Choose a manageable group of samples and use a separate, clearly labelled fragrance blotter for each. Follow the supplied product instructions. Record how you applied each sample and keep the blotters separate so that your notes refer to the intended product.

Decide when you will revisit the samples and write down the actual observation times. Compare them in a similar setting, noting changes such as moving rooms or returning after a break. If the application method or surroundings differ, record the difference instead of presenting the observations as directly equivalent.

Describe what you notice in ordinary language. “Still noticeable on this blotter at this observation time” is more useful than a broad claim that the perfume lasts all day. If you also record personal wear observations from normal use, keep them separate from the blotter notes and identify the context. Neither record replaces product testing or supports a universal performance claim.

### Keep individual feedback before discussing the result

If several buyers or colleagues compare the same shortlist, ask each person to record their impression before discussing a shared decision. Keep differing preferences visible. Your aim is to understand the assortment you want to buy, not to invent a unanimous test result.

## Check the sample's physical presentation

Inspect the parcel and supplied components when they arrive. Photograph the item reference and any visible damage before separating the packaging. For a finished bottle, record the sprayer's observed operation, cap fit, visible leakage and the condition of the bottle and carton.

Send a specific question when something is unclear. “The sample arrived without an outer carton; is a carton included in the quoted retail unit?” gives the supplier something concrete to resolve. Do not silently assume that a sample's presentation is the final bulk specification.

## Turn observations into a buying decision

Shortlist a sample when you can explain its place in your assortment and identify the remaining commercial questions. Revisit it when the sample reference, package contents or comparison conditions are unclear. Decline it when it does not fit the customer or presentation you are planning.

Before placing a wholesale order, link the selected sample to a written product specification and quote. Confirm the exact product, quantity, supplied packaging, price basis, current availability and dispatch arrangement. Do not treat sample approval as approval of a different size, fragrance or packaging version.

## Questions buyers often ask

### Does a sample prove how long a perfume will last?

Your record describes what you observed in the stated conditions. It does not prove a fixed wear duration for every person or setting. Ask for relevant product-specific evidence before using a performance claim in your own sales material.

### Can I evaluate packaging from a fragrance vial?

A vial does not show the retail bottle, sprayer or carton unless those components are also supplied. Request the appropriate product presentation and confirm any differences before approving an order.

### Does paying for a sample reserve wholesale stock?

Do not assume it does. Confirm stock allocation, the final order quantity and the applicable order terms separately with your quotation.
