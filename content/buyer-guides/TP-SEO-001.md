# Custom Logo Perfume: A Private Label Buying Guide

A perfume with your logo starts with a few practical decisions: which product you want to sell, where your branding should appear, and what needs to be approved before production. Getting those decisions clear makes the first quotation more useful—and gives you a better basis for comparing options.

This guide is for retailers and new beauty brands planning a fragrance project. It explains how to prepare a brief, separate different types of customization, and move from an idea to a sample and quotation.

## Choose the buying route that matches your plan

Begin by deciding whether you want to purchase finished products for resale or develop a product under your own brand. The available products, minimum quantities and approval steps can differ.

| Buying route | What you are asking for | What to confirm first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished-product wholesale | An existing product for your retail assortment | Exact product reference, quantity, packaging and current availability |
| Your logo on an eligible product | Your brand identity applied through an agreed branding route | Product eligibility, logo position, available method and minimum quantity |
| A wider development project | Changes to packaging, fragrance direction or other product details | Feasible scope, samples, separate costs and project timing |

## Decide where your logo should appear

Think about how a customer will see and handle the product. The bottle is one part of the presentation; the carton, label and other agreed packaging elements may also carry your brand information.

Prepare a simple placement list. For each part, identify what you want to show: the brand name, product name, design element or other approved text. Ask which branding methods are available for the selected component before fixing the artwork.

Questions worth resolving early include:

- Is the proposed logo position suitable for this bottle or packaging surface?
- What usable area and artwork dimensions should the designer work to?
- Which colours and finishes are available for this specific option?
- Will the quotation cover the bottle branding, outer box or both?
- What additional product information must be accommodated in the final artwork for the intended market?

Treat a reference image as a way to explain your preferred direction. The agreed specification and approved sample should identify what will actually be supplied.

## Prepare the product brief before polishing the design

A useful first message describes the business and product decisions as well as the logo. You can begin while some choices are still open: mark them as questions so the quotation addresses them directly.

| Brief field | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Business and customers | Your shop or brand, target customers and sales channel |
| Product direction | Perfume, fragrance mist or another requested format; preferred size |
| Fragrance direction | Your description of the scent you want to explore, if relevant |
| Branding scope | Bottle, label, carton or other requested elements |
| Quantity | Estimated units, with a breakdown by product or version |
| Artwork status | Logo available, artwork in progress, or guidance requested |
| Destination | Country and city for the shipping discussion |
| Timing | Your preferred launch date and any fixed deadline |
| Budget | A purchasing budget or target cost, if you have one |

For example, “one fragrance product with our logo on the bottle and carton” is a clearer starting scope than “fully custom perfume.” It allows the supplier to explain what is available and which decisions still affect the quotation.

## Confirm how the minimum quantity is counted

Ask whether the minimum applies per fragrance, bottle size, packaging version or artwork design. A quantity spread across several versions may be treated differently from the same quantity of one confirmed item.

TopPerfume’s current route page lists selected logo and private-label options from 100 pieces. This is a starting point for eligible projects. Ask for the minimum quantity and available configuration for the exact product you are discussing.

Also ask what changes if you revise the bottle, fragrance, decoration or outer packaging. A finished-product wholesale minimum should not be used to budget a different custom project.

## Review artwork and samples in separate steps

An artwork layout helps you review placement and text. A physical sample allows you to inspect the supplied materials and appearance. Ask which type of sample is available at each stage and what it represents.

Before approving the project, review:

- The correct brand spelling, product wording and agreed artwork version.
- Logo size, position and appearance on the selected component.
- The bottle, cap and packaging configuration included in the quotation.
- The selected fragrance sample or product reference, where applicable.
- Any differences between the sample and the proposed bulk order.

Keep the approval record specific. Use a dated reference and an agreed list of components, rather than relying on a general “looks good” message. Ask for sample charges and shipping costs to be stated before proceeding.

## Request a quotation with an agreed scope

Your quotation should identify the product, quantity, branding work and packaging included. Ask whether there are separate artwork, setup, sample or shipping charges, and which costs remain to be confirmed.

Discuss the timeline as stages: specification confirmation, artwork approval, sample review and dispatch planning. The applicable timing depends on the selected project. A general website dispatch indication should not become a fixed arrival promise for an unconfirmed custom order.

For repeat orders, ask how the approved specification will be referenced and whether availability, quantity requirements or pricing need to be reconfirmed.

## Questions buyers often ask

### Can I start by adding a logo without developing a new fragrance?

Ask about the existing options eligible for your own branding. Your logo brief can be reviewed separately from a new fragrance-development request; availability is confirmed by product and supplier.

### Do I need finished artwork before contacting you?

You can start with a product direction, estimated quantity and preferred logo position. State whether your logo is ready and request the applicable artwork requirements before preparing the final layout.

### Can I split the order across several designs?

Include the proposed quantity for each version in your brief. The minimum and setup arrangements must be checked for that configuration.

### Does a catalogue product automatically support my own logo?

Customization eligibility is reviewed separately. Identify the product or format you like and ask which private-label options are suitable.
